## Original Query
Let's do that, write a new Step 1 for an exploratory paper.

## Understood Intent
Produce a useful exploratory paper sooner by drafting alongside the remaining historical reviews, with one approval cycle and internal batches of about 30 instead of repeating all four planning steps. Preserve room for later refinement and stronger evaluation.

## Problem
Repeated planning cycles delay the manuscript, while waiting for exhaustive adjudication or causal evidence would postpone a defensible descriptive paper unnecessarily.

## Evidence and Boundary
The [census](../../research/historical_cycle_expansion/coverage.md) contains 349 candidates: 216 eligible, 95 uncertain/unfinished, two excluded research cycles, seven duplicates/companions and 29 context exclusions. The [first analysis](../../research/historical_outcome_analysis/report.md) reviewed 25 eligible records; 191 remain. Its judgments and [corrections](../../research/historical_outcome_analysis/corrections.md) are documentary evidence, not independently verified success rates. Preserve original sources and study cutoffs.

## Option A — Finish reviews before writing
Review all remaining eligible candidates, reconcile findings, then write the paper.

- **Pros:** Stable results before drafting; fewer manuscript revisions.
- **Cons:** Delays a readable paper and feedback on its framing; can expand into indefinite evidence gathering.

## Option B — Draft early, refine through batches
Create a complete provisional manuscript from existing evidence, then review all 191 remaining eligible candidates under one plan, updating results and interpretation after each batch.

- **Pros:** Delivers a paper early; preserves full-coverage intent and traceable refinement without repeated planning gates.
- **Cons:** Requires explicit versioning, provisional counts and careful separation of completed analysis from queued work.

## Option C — Finish a preliminary paper from the first 25
Write a narrowly framed paper now and leave the remaining reviews to future work.

- **Pros:** Fastest finished draft; clear small scope.
- **Cons:** Queue-order selection is not representative; leaves the requested broader review unfinished and limits conclusions.

## Recommendation
Choose **Option B**: one research-and-paper cycle with reviewable batch checkpoints.

- **Early deliverable:** A coherent local manuscript with abstract, motivation/workflow, research questions, methods, current results, discussion, limitations and future work. State “25 of 216 reviewed” initially; preserve unknowns and cite the evidence. Verify literature references before using them; do not invent citations or claim novelty without support.
- **Execution:** Review the remaining 191 in seven batches of at most 30, following the existing queue and shared rubric; reuse the first 25 with corrections. Each batch updates evidence, cumulative coverage and manuscript claims, with verification and a stage commit. No new four-step cycle per batch. The user's accepted direction authorizes an exception to the usual one-day/eight-stage ceiling for this research effort; retain approval and scope checks for material changes.
- **Final handoff:** A reconciled exploratory manuscript covering all 216 eligible records, linked evidence, limitations and a refinement/review checklist. Fix the paper's dated corpus boundary; retain all 95 adjudication IDs and explain exclusion/selection bias without making their resolution a prerequisite. No difficult eligible record is silently dropped.
- **Claims and release:** Describe retained practice, reported outcomes, unmet gates, corrections and evidence gaps. No causal effectiveness or time-saving claims. Human coding review remains pending until performed; identify central/disputed claims and excerpts needing review before public release. Drafting does not authorize publication. Experiments, exhaustive adjudication and product changes remain later work.
